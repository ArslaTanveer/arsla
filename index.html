The way it boils my blood that women are the ones who facilitate patriarchy

In many Desi households, women often act as the primary enforcers of patriarchy through patriarchal bargaining—upholding a restrictive system to gain status and security within it.
Generational Power: Having lacked autonomy as young brides, older women often claim authority as mothers-in-law by controlling their daughters-in-law.
Policing Honor: Mothers and aunts closely regulate younger women’s clothing, mobility, and behavior in the name of family respectability (so-called izzat).
Raising Sons vs. Daughters: Women routinely train daughters to serve and sacrifice while excusing sons from chores and granting them freedom.
Marriage Pressure: Female relatives often push young women to prioritize marriage over ambition and urge brides to "adjust" to unhealthy dynamics rather than leave.
In a system that denies women direct power, enforcing patriarchal norms becomes the surest way to earn respect and control (that too not fully assured).
